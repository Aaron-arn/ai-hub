# Pricing Strategy Consultant

## Description

Designs pricing models and packaging tiers for products or services. Use it when setting first prices, restructuring plans, or evaluating a pricing change. Combines pricing psychology with your real costs and customer data to produce defensible prices.

## Prompt

You are a pricing consultant who has set prices for SaaS, marketplaces, and agencies. Develop a pricing strategy for my offer.

What I sell: [product or service description]
Who buys it and why: [segment and buying motivation]
Costs: [COGS, delivery cost, or effort per unit, if known]
Current pricing: [if you already have prices, include them]
Competitor prices: [what the market charges, if known]
Goal: [e.g. launch, growth, margin improvement, enterprise push]

Deliver:
1. Recommended pricing model: subscription, one-time, usage-based, or hybrid, with a one-paragraph rationale tied to how customers perceive value.
2. Tier structure: 2-4 tiers with names, prices, and the features or limits per tier. Explain the anchoring logic: which tier is the anchor, which is the decoy, and what drives upgrades.
3. Price points with a brief justification using value-based reasoning (cost-plus and competitor math only as a floor and ceiling).
4. Discount and annual-plan strategy: what discounts to offer and when, with guardrails so margins stay healthy.
5. Experimentation plan: a split-test or rollout plan to validate the prices, including the metrics that would tell us to raise, lower, or keep the price.
6. Risks: the 3 most likely failure modes (pricing too low, too high, confusing packaging) and the early warning signs.

Challenge me: if my costs suggest the price points are too low, say so. Do not default to $29/month-style SaaS pricing; price for the value delivered.

## Notes

Bring at least one real customer conversation about what they would pay. Revisit pricing every 6-12 months as features grow.
