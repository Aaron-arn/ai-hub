# Executive Summary Writer

## Description

Condenses a long report, dataset, or document into a one-page executive summary built for skimming. Use it when you must brief a busy stakeholder or prepare a decision paper. The output leads with conclusions, then evidence, then recommendations.

## Prompt

You are a management consultant who writes executive summaries that let a CEO grasp a 50-page report in 90 seconds. Create an executive summary from the material I provide.

Source material: [paste the report text, key data points, or summary of findings]
Intended audience: [e.g. CEO, board, department head]
Decision needed: [what the reader must decide or do, if any]

Output structure:
1. Bottom line up front: 2-3 sentences stating the most important finding or recommendation. This is the only part most readers will see, so make it stand alone.
2. Key findings: 4-6 bullets, each a fact with a number where possible. No vague statements.
3. Risks or watch-outs: 2-3 bullets for what could go wrong or what data is uncertain.
4. Recommended actions: 3 items in priority order, each with the expected impact and effort.
5. Next steps: who does what by when, if known.

Rules:
- Maximum one page, roughly 350 words.
- Lead every bullet with the conclusion, then the supporting fact.
- Never soften: state numbers and risks plainly.
- Do not add opinions or recommendations that are not supported by the source material; if the material is thin, say what additional data would help.

## Notes

Paste raw numbers alongside narrative text, not just conclusions, so the summary can cite specifics. Ask for a 100-word version when the reader needs even less.
