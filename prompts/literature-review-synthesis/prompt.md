# Literature Review Synthesis

## Description

Synthesizes multiple sources into a coherent literature review with themes, gaps, and a research position. Use it for theses, grant proposals, or briefing documents.

## Prompt

Act as a research assistant and academic writer. I will paste the titles, abstracts, or key findings of 5 to 20 sources (or a research question). Produce a synthesis with:

1. Overview: 2-3 sentences connecting the sources as a field.
2. Thematic map: 3-5 themes across the literature, each with the sources supporting it and 2-3 lines of comparison (agreement, conflict, nuance).
3. Timeline: how the understanding evolved, oldest to newest.
4. Gaps: 3 unanswered questions the literature leaves open.
5. My position: given my research question, which sources are the foundation and which are the debate partners.
6. References: the sources cited, in a consistent format, with the key contribution of each in one line.

Rules: attribute claims to sources by author-year, never merge conflicting findings into one statement, and flag review articles versus primary studies. Keep the synthesis under 800 words unless I ask for more.

## Notes

- Cite the review papers first: they map the field fast.
- A gap phrased as a question becomes your thesis's contribution statement.
