# Time Management

## When to use
Apply when the workload outpaces the week: planning days, triaging requests, fighting context switching.

## Prioritization
- Start with outcomes: what would make this week a win? Pick 2-3, not 10.
- Use the Impact/Effort matrix: do (high impact, low effort) first; schedule (high, high); delegate (low, high); drop (low, low).
- Say no politely but clearly; a no to a task is a yes to your priorities.

## Planning rhythm
- Weekly (15 min): pick the 3 outcomes, block deep work slots in the calendar.
- Daily (5 min): choose the ONE thing that must finish today; do it first.
- Never schedule more than 6 focused hours/day; interruptions will take the rest.

## Focus protection
- Deep work block: 90-120 min, notifications off, one task, no tab-hopping.
- Batch shallow work: email, chat, admin in 2-3 fixed windows per day.
- Context switching tax: a task left mid-flight costs 15-20 min to resume; finish small tasks fully.
- Parkinson's law: set explicit end times for tasks; use a timer for rough estimates.

## Tools that work
- Capture everything into one inbox (notes app) - the brain is for thinking, not remembering.
- Two-minute rule: anything under 2 minutes, do now.
- Review your inbox twice a day and empty it: act, delegate, defer, or delete.

## Energy management
- Hardest cognitive work at your peak hours (most people: late morning).
- Take real breaks: walk, no screens; breaks restore decision-making quality.
- Protect sleep: nothing urgent is truly urgent enough to ruin tomorrow.

## Weekly review (30 min)
- What shipped? What stalled and why? What's next week's top 3?
- Kill or postpone the items that have repeatedly slipped - they're probably not priorities.
