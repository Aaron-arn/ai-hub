# UX Research

## When to use
Apply when making product decisions that depend on user behavior: feature prioritization, flow redesigns, copy changes.

## Choose the method for the question
- What do users do? -> Analytics, session replays.
- Why do they do it? -> Interviews, diary studies.
- Does the design work? -> Usability tests, moderated sessions.
- Which option is best? -> A/B test, preference test.
- What should we build? -> Jobs-to-be-done interviews, competitive teardown.

## Interview skill
- Ask about concrete past behavior, not hypotheticals: "last time you X, walk me through it".
- Never ask leading questions ("did you find it confusing?") or yes/no questions without follow-up.
- Five whys to reach the root need; mirror the user's own words.
- 5-8 participants per round finds most issues; do rounds, not one big study.

## Usability test recipe
1. Write 3-5 tasks that cover the critical journey, each with a success definition.
2. Ask users to think aloud; you are silent, supportive, non-guiding.
3. Measure: completion, time, errors, frustration cues.
4. Debrief with a 2-question survey (SUS or one net-promoter-style question).
5. Report: per-task success rate, top 5 issues by severity, quotes as evidence.

## Bias controls
- Recruit outside your team and friends-of-the-team.
- Vary participants: skill level, tenure, device.
- Watch your own bias: decide in advance what evidence would change your mind.

## Output
- Research brief -> findings (what users said/did, with evidence) -> recommendations (decision-ready).
- Tag findings by confidence: observed multiple times / once / self-reported.
- Always state the method and sample size so the audience can weigh the evidence.
