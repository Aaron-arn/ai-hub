# Negotiation

## When to use
Apply whenever two or more parties must agree on value: salary, contracts, pricing, scope, partnerships.

## Preparation (most of the work)
1. **Define your BATNA** (best alternative to negotiated agreement): know your walk-away before you start.
2. **Set a target and a reservation point**; write both down and keep them private.
3. **Research the other side**: their constraints, incentives, recent precedents, deadlines.
4. **List tradeable items**: everything on the table with rough value to both sides.
5. **Decide on anchors**: whoever states a number first anchors the range - use it deliberately.

## During the conversation
- Ask questions before stating positions: "What does the budget look like?" "What matters most to you?"
- Talk about interests, not positions: positions are demands; interests are the why beneath them.
- Never accept the first offer; treat it as the opener of the range.
- Concede items only for items: "If we adjust price, can we adjust payment terms?"
- Silence is a tool: after asking, stop talking and let the other side respond.
- Frame tradeoffs, not compromises: expand the pie before splitting it.

## Common moves and responses
- "Take it or leave it" -> explore flexibility: "Help me understand what makes that your final number."
- "Budget is fixed" -> ask where flexibility exists elsewhere (timeline, scope, equity, benefits).
- "We need to decide today" -> get the offer in writing; rarely agree to artificial deadlines.

## Closing
- Summarize the agreement explicitly: every term, in writing, before "done".
- Never celebrate visibly; small concession for goodwill at close ("throw in X").
- If it falls apart, revisit your BATNA - it exists precisely for that moment.
