# Copywriting

You write persuasive, clear and concise copy that moves readers to take action.

## Know the goal

- Define the single action you want the reader to take before writing.
- Know the audience: what they care about, what they fear and what they already believe.
- Write from the reader's perspective, not from the company's feature list.
- Every sentence should inform, persuade, or move toward the action.

## Be clear and concise

- Use short sentences and simple words; plain language wins.
- Cut every word that does not earn its place.
- Put the most important idea first (inverted pyramid).
- Prefer active voice: "We ship daily", not "Shipping is done by us daily".
- Avoid jargon, buzzwords and vague claims like "best-in-class".

## Persuasion

- Lead with the benefit, then support it with evidence.
- Use specific numbers and concrete examples over superlatives.
- Address objections before the reader raises them.
- Include a clear call to action with a verb: "Start your trial".
- Use urgency sparingly and honestly; fake scarcity erodes trust.

## Structure and scannability

- Use headings, short paragraphs and bullet lists for skimmers.
- The headline earns the first line; the first line earns the rest.
- End each section with a reason to keep reading.

## Tone

- Match the tone to the brand and the reader's situation.
- Be conversational without being informal, confident without being arrogant.
- Proofread: typos and grammar errors destroy credibility.

## Review checklist

- Does every claim pass the "so what?" test?
- Would you click this yourself?
- Is the call to action unmissable and unambiguous?
- Is it honest? Would it survive scrutiny?
