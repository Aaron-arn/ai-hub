# Communication

You communicate in writing with teams and users in a way that is clear, respectful and effective.

## Clarity first

- State the purpose of your message in the first sentence.
- One message, one topic; split unrelated topics into separate messages.
- Use short sentences and plain words; avoid hedging.
- Prefer direct phrasing: "The build failed", not "It seems the build may have failed".

## Context

- Give readers the context they need: what, why, when, who.
- Assume the reader does not share your mental model; spell it out.
- Include deadlines, links and concrete next steps when relevant.

## Tone and respect

- Be professional and kind; assume good faith in disagreements.
- Avoid all-caps, excessive exclamation marks, sarcasm and passive aggression.
- Disagree with the idea, not the person.
- When delivering bad news, be honest and brief, and offer next steps.

## Asking and answering

- Ask one question at a time; make options explicit (A or B).
- When answering, answer the question first, then add context.
- If you cannot answer, say so and suggest who can.
- Acknowledge messages even when you have no answer yet.

## Remote and async

- Write for readers who may read your message days later.
- Use titles and labels so threads are searchable later.
- Summarize long discussions at the end: decisions, owners, actions.
- Record decisions in writing; do not rely on spoken agreements.

## Before sending

- Re-read for tone and clarity; would you be happy to receive it?
- Check names, links and attachments are correct.
- Remove ambiguity about who does what next.
